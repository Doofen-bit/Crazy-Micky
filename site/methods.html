What the views mean.

Stress Atlas is an Information Visualization course project by Yixuan Du (yd2927), Yi Yao (yy5075), and Jinyu Zheng (jz6539), NYU Shanghai, Fall 2026. It supports three tasks: compare contexts, inspect a candidate gene with individual variability, and explore shared functional annotations.

In plain words

  1. The experiment. Male mice were stressed for 15 days by daily encounters with a larger, aggressive mouse. A social test then sorted them into resilient (still sociable) and susceptible (avoidant) groups; control mice were never stressed. Brains were sampled at four times of day (ZT4, ZT10 lights on; ZT16, ZT22 lights off) from two regions: the SCN (master clock) and the habenula (processes bad experiences).
  2. The measurement. RNA-seq counts how many RNA messages each of ~46,000 genes produced in each brain sample: a read-out of gene activity.
  3. Our analysis. For every region × time combination we compared groups gene by gene (DESeq2), giving 24 comparisons. A gene counts as “changed” when it is both confident (adjusted p below the cutoff) and big enough (fold change above the cutoff).
  4. The meaning. We then asked which known biological processes (Gene Ontology) contain more changed genes than chance would predict.
  5. The website. Four linked views: where/when changes happen → which genes changed → how one gene behaves in every mouse across the day → which processes the changed genes share. Nothing is re-computed in the browser; it only filters prepared results.

Data and scope

We use gene-level RNA-seq counts released by Narain et al. (2024), GIN DOI 10.12751/g-node.jj57l9. The experiment sampled male mice with control, stress-resilient, and stress-susceptible phenotypes in SCN and habenula (Hb) at ZT4, ZT10, ZT16, and ZT22. Hb is the display name for source files labeled LHb; we do not assert pure lateral habenula tissue.

The supplied archive contains 96 real count files, each with 46,603 Ensembl gene IDs. FASTQ and some processed files are annex pointer stubs; they are not used. We retain 93 tissue samples from 47 animals after the author's three documented exclusions. A complete count matrix and per-file hashes are audited offline.

Reanalysis, adapted from classroom code

  1. Read nonnegative integer counts and align metadata by unique Ensembl and sample IDs. Keep source IDs, corrected analysis IDs, and animal IDs separately.
  2. Estimate DESeq2 median-ratio size factors across included samples within each brain region. Use these same factors for stratum models and time profiles.
  3. Within each region × time stratum, keep genes with a median raw count of at least 10 in any phenotype. Fit ~ phenotype using all three groups. Extract resilient/control, susceptible/control, and resilient/susceptible contrasts: eight models, 24 comparisons.
  4. Use unshrunken Wald log₂ fold changes; BH-adjusted p-values retain DESeq2 independent filtering and Cook's-distance behavior. Independent filtering uses α = .05. Default displayed DEGs require p-adj < .05 and |log₂FC| > .5; these are display cutoffs, not an effect-size null test. No multiplicity correction is applied across the 24 separate contrasts.
  5. Export full precision statistics with NA as JSON null. A gene filtered out of a contrast still has its raw-derived normalized expression available.

Classroom source: Genomics/gb_RNAseq_student.Rmd. The original lesson used Arabidopsis, two groups, default p-adj < .1 and |log₂FC| > 1. Our species mapping, three-group fits, normalization scope and display thresholds are explicit adaptations. Browser threshold changes never rerun independent filtering.

GO network and enrichment

Mouse Ensembl IDs are mapped with org.Mm.eg.db; GO.db supplies names. Exact package versions and source dates are stored in the manifest and annotation metadata. GOALL includes inherited Biological Process memberships. Ambiguous symbols use a deterministic display choice; Ensembl IDs remain the join key.

We implement the classroom hypergeometric overrepresentation idea in Python/SciPy: foreground = GO-mapped genes passing the default .05/.5 cutoffs; background = GO-mapped genes with finite adjusted p-values in that contrast. Terms with 10–500 background genes are tested, including zero overlaps. BH correction includes every tested term within a contrast. This is not a claimed clusterProfiler execution. Full term results, denominators and foreground sizes are exported.

The network shows up to five enriched terms and 30 genes ranked by adjusted p-value. If no terms are enriched, it shows explicitly labeled annotation-only processes shared by top-ranked genes. A selected process prioritizes its members in the bounded view and highlights all its represented members in the volcano. A selected gene without a displayed membership remains an isolated node. Network position has no quantitative meaning. Edges encode membership, never regulation.

Read the charts precisely

  • Overview: color and numbers show DEG counts at common selected cutoffs; the scale is shared within the displayed eight cells. n labels refer to the two contrasted groups. Counts do not measure effect size and depend on power and the tested universe.
  • Volcano: x = log₂ fold change, y = −log₁₀ adjusted p-value. Missing p-adj genes cannot be placed on y. Values of zero are capped only for display at 300.
  • Expression: points = log₂(normalized count + 1); line = log₂(arithmetic mean normalized count + 1). Shapes and line patterns distinguish groups. Lines do not follow the same animal across time.
  • Network: GO enrichment is fixed at default cutoffs even when exploration cutoffs change. Annotation overlap is not enrichment.

Limitations and review

Small and unequal groups, one dataset, male mice, bulk tissue composition and possible unmodeled batch effects limit conclusions. QC PCA uses the top 500 variable genes after log₂ normalization; it is descriptive, not a new exclusion rule. A significant result at one time and a nonsignificant result at another is not a formal interaction test. Four samples of clock time do not establish rhythmicity. Associations in mice do not establish mechanisms or treatment effects in humans.
